// Findings

## Atmospheric Reality Gap

The four images attached tell a genuinely strong visual story: a dynamic school exterior with motion-blurred students conveying life in the building, a dusk-lit urban retail façade in Seattle with warm amber tones and street-level energy, a clean aerial of a mixed-use residential tower establishing scale, and an interior pool shot where the mural reflection on still water creates a compositionally distinctive frame. These are competent, professional images — the kind a regional GC with 60 years of history should be showing.

The trust cost is not the photography. It is that a buyer who clicks any internal link to see more of this work hits a dead end — literally. The images earn attention; the site infrastructure immediately destroys it.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

11 of 13 homepage links are broken. That is not a maintenance issue — that is a portfolio that does not exist to anyone who tries to verify it. The links to `/projects/`, `/about/`, `/services/`, `/careers/`, and `/contact/` all return unreachable. The project-specific URL `bayley.net/project/seattle-waterfront-retrooms/` is also dead. A design-build decision-maker comparing two GCs at the shortlist stage clicks through to validate scope and experience. At Bayley, every click confirms the same thing: nobody is watching this. That perception — a 60-year firm that cannot keep its own website functional — transfers directly onto the question of whether they can manage a complex, multi-phase commercial project.

The homepage copy promises "hundreds of award-winning projects" and work for "high-profile clients nationwide." The four hero images start to substantiate that. The broken project archive erases it entirely. You are running a credential claim with no evidence trail behind it.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

All 21 content images on the homepage carry zero alt text — 100% coverage gap. Search engines cannot index a single project type, location, or building category from your image library. For a GC competing on project type and geography across 12 states, every untagged image is a missed ranking signal for searches like "commercial GC Seattle" or "multifamily contractor Pacific Northwest."

The `og:image` tag is missing entirely. When anyone shares a Bayley project link — a subcontractor, a past client, a prospect — the social card renders blank. No image, no context, no click. That is lost referral traffic from the exact peer-to-peer sharing that drives commercial construction vendor discovery.

The page title "Homepage - Bayley Construction" at 30 characters wastes the most valuable SEO real estate on the page. Your ranking #1 for your own brand name means you own branded searches — buyers who already know you. The metadata does nothing to capture the unbranded searches where new project opportunities actually originate.

Fix the broken links first — that is the single action that converts your existing photography from decoration into a working business asset. Then the images can do what they are already capable of doing.
